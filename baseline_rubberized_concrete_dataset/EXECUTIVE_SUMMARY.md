## What This Dataset Provides

This is a **comprehensive baseline characterization dataset** for rubberized concrete designed for fire-resistant structural applications. It establishes the **"BEFORE"** state - complete material properties at ambient temperature (23°C) that serve as the foundation for subsequent high-temperature testing and thermo-mechanical modeling.

### Dataset Scope

**4 Concrete Mixtures:**
- RC-00: Control mix (0% rubber)
- RC-05: 5% rubber replacement
- RC-10: 10% rubber replacement  
- RC-15: 15% rubber replacement

**18 Data Files** containing:
- ✅ Complete mixture proportions and constituent properties
- ✅ Rubber characterization (physical, chemical, thermal)
- ✅ Fresh state properties (workability, setting)
- ✅ Mechanical properties (strength, modulus, ductility)
- ✅ Physical properties (density, porosity, pore structure)
- ✅ Microstructure (SEM, XRD, ITZ analysis)
- ✅ Thermal properties (conductivity, specific heat, expansion)
- ✅ Durability indicators (permeability, freeze-thaw, chloride)
- ✅ Complete stress-strain curves

**>2000 Individual Measurements** from **100+ Specimens** tested according to ASTM/BS/ISO standards.

## Key Scientific Findings

### 1. Mechanical Performance Trade-offs

**Strength Reduction:**
- 5% rubber: -10.5% compressive strength (52.3 → 46.8 MPa)
- 10% rubber: -23.9% compressive strength (52.3 → 39.8 MPa)
- 15% rubber: -37.9% compressive strength (52.3 → 32.5 MPa)

**Ductility Enhancement:**
- 5% rubber: +25% ductility index
- 10% rubber: +70% ductility index
- 15% rubber: +140% ductility index

**Key Insight:** Rubber systematically trades strength for ductility. The optimal rubber content depends on application requirements.

### 2. Failure Mode Transformation

**Control Mix (RC-00):**
- Brittle cone-and-shear failure
- Sudden load drop at 2850 microstrain
- Minimal post-peak capacity

**15% Rubber Mix (RC-15):**
- Highly ductile progressive crushing
- Extended deformation to 6850 microstrain (2.4× control)
- Significant residual strength maintained
- Rubber particles bridge cracks and prevent catastrophic failure

**Significance for Fire Resistance:** Enhanced ductility may accommodate thermal strains and reduce explosive spalling risk during fire exposure.

### 3. Weak Interface Zone is the Culprit

**SEM/XRD Analysis Reveals:**
- **ITZ thickness around rubber:** 95-165 μm (vs. 45 μm for normal aggregates)
- **ITZ porosity around rubber:** 35-58% (vs. 18% for normal ITZ)
- **Physical gaps:** 5-22 μm separation between rubber and cement matrix
- **Rubber surface coverage:** Only 35-65% of rubber surface has cement paste adhesion

**Conclusion:** Poor rubber-cement bonding is the primary mechanism for strength reduction. Pre-treatment (NaOH wash) helps but doesn't fully solve the problem.

### 4. Porosity Evolution

**Total Porosity Progression:**
- RC-00: 12.8%
- RC-05: 14.5% (+13%)
- RC-10: 16.8% (+31%)
- RC-15: 19.5% (+52%)

**Critical Finding at 15% Rubber:**
- Interconnected pore network develops (percolation threshold)
- Large interfacial voids (>50 μm) become dominant
- Mercury intrusion shows dramatic increase in macro-pores

**Significance:** Higher porosity increases permeability (durability concern) but may provide pressure relief pathways during fire (spalling reduction).

### 5. Rubber Thermal Decomposition

**TGA Analysis (25-800°C):**
- **Initial degradation:** 200-250°C (plasticizers volatilize)
- **Main decomposition:** 300-400°C (peak mass loss rate at 350°C)
- **Peak degradation rate:** 42.8% per °C at 400°C
- **Total mass loss:** ~87% at 800°C (13% ash remains)

**Products Released:**
- Styrene, butadiene (monomers)
- Aromatic hydrocarbons
- CO, CO₂ (combustion gases)

**Critical Implication:** During fire exposure, rubber will decompose and create:
1. Internal porosity (volume previously occupied by rubber)
2. Combustible gases (fire load concern)
3. Internal pressure (potential driving force for spalling OR relief if porosity allows escape)

### 6. Thermal Insulation Enhancement

**Thermal Conductivity Reduction:**
- RC-00: 1.82 W/(m·K)
- RC-15: 1.12 W/(m·K) (-38%)

**Benefit:** Slower temperature rise through concrete cross-section during fire exposure.

**Specific Heat Increase:**
- RC-00: 880 J/(kg·K)
- RC-15: 1015 J/(kg·K) (+15%)

**Benefit:** Higher heat absorption capacity.

**Combined Effect:** Thermal diffusivity drops by 43%, meaning rubberized concrete heats up significantly slower than normal concrete.

### 7. Durability Trade-offs

**Degraded Properties:**
- Water permeability: +4.4× at 15% rubber
- Chloride penetration depth: +3.3×
- Carbonation depth: +3.3×

**Improved Properties:**
- Freeze-thaw mass loss: -75% (much better)
- Scaling resistance: -66% (much better)
- Impact resistance: Significantly enhanced (from ductility)

**Conclusion:** Rubberized concrete shows reduced penetration resistance but enhanced physical damage resistance.

## Practical Implications

### For Structural Engineers

**When to Use Rubberized Concrete:**
- ✅ Applications requiring ductility (seismic zones, impact resistance)
- ✅ Lightweight construction (8% density reduction at 15% rubber)
- ✅ Thermal insulation (38% conductivity reduction)
- ✅ Freeze-thaw exposure (improved damage resistance)
- ✅ Potentially: Fire-resistant structures (pending high-temp validation)

**When NOT to Use:**
- ❌ High-strength requirements (f'c > 40 MPa needs ≤5% rubber)
- ❌ Aggressive chemical environments (high permeability)
- ❌ Marine structures (chloride penetration concern)

**Design Recommendations:**
- **5% rubber:** Minimal strength loss (-10%), good ductility gain (+25%)
- **10% rubber:** Moderate strength loss (-24%), significant ductility (+70%)
- **15% rubber:** High strength loss (-38%), exceptional ductility (+140%)

### For Researchers

**This Dataset Enables:**
1. **Thermo-mechanical model development:** Complete baseline material properties
2. **Fire resistance prediction:** TGA data for decomposition kinetics; porosity for vapor transport
3. **Microstructure-property relationships:** Quantified ITZ effects
4. **Multi-scale modeling:** Data from nano (C-S-H) to macro (structural) scales
5. **Validation at ambient temperature:** Comprehensive test results for model verification

**Critical Data for Fire Modeling:**
- Initial porosity + rubber TGA → predict evolved porosity during heating
- Initial mechanical properties + temperature degradation → predict residual capacity
- Thermal properties → predict temperature distribution
- Microstructure → inform damage mechanisms

### For Mix Design Optimization

**Key Parameters Identified:**
- **Water-cement ratio:** 0.45 maintained across all mixes (constant)
- **Superplasticizer dosage:** Increase from 0.8% to 1.5% to maintain workability
- **Rubber particle size:** 1-4 mm (smaller particles may improve bonding)
- **Pre-treatment:** NaOH wash improves adhesion but ITZ still weak
- **Replacement method:** Volume replacement of fine aggregate (mass replacement would differ)

**Future Optimization Opportunities:**
- Surface modification of rubber (silanes, plasma treatment)
- Hybrid replacement (combine rubber with other lightweight aggregates)
- Nano-silica addition to densify ITZ
- Different rubber sizes (optimize particle size distribution)
