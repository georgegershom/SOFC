# Methodology Documentation: Food Processing Firms FDI Research Dataset

## Research Framework

### Research Title
**The Influence of Foreign Direct Investment on the Performance of Food Processing Firms in Lagos, Nigeria: The Moderating Role of the Nigerian Government Policy**

### Research Objectives
1. Examine the direct relationship between FDI and food processing firm performance
2. Investigate the mechanisms through which FDI influences performance (knowledge absorption, innovation, task performance)
3. Analyze the moderating role of Nigerian government policy on the FDI-performance relationship
4. Provide policy recommendations for enhancing FDI benefits in the food processing sector

---

## 1. RESEARCH DESIGN

### 1.1 Study Type
- **Design**: Cross-sectional survey with secondary data integration
- **Approach**: Mixed-methods (quantitative primary with quantitative secondary)
- **Time Frame**: Cross-sectional data collection (2024) with retrospective performance data (2022-2024)
- **Geographic Scope**: Lagos State, Nigeria

### 1.2 Theoretical Framework
- **Base Theory**: Resource-Based View (RBV) and Dynamic Capabilities Theory
- **FDI Theory**: Internalization Theory and Eclectic Paradigm (Dunning)
- **Moderation Theory**: Institutional Theory and Policy Environment Framework

## 2. SAMPLING METHODOLOGY

### 2.1 Population Definition
- **Target Population**: Food processing firms operating in Lagos State, Nigeria
- **Sector Definition**: NACE Code 10 (Manufacture of food products)
- **Geographic Boundary**: Lagos State (including Lagos Island, Lagos Mainland, and Epe divisions)
- **Time Boundary**: Firms operational as of December 2023

### 2.2 Sampling Frame
- **Source**: Lagos State Ministry of Commerce and Industry business registry
- **Additional Sources**: Manufacturers Association of Nigeria (MAN) Lagos chapter
- **Population Size**: Approximately 450 registered food processing firms
- **Eligibility Criteria**:
  - Minimum 10 employees (to exclude micro enterprises)
  - Operational for at least 2 years
  - Primary business in food processing/manufacturing
  - Willing to participate in research

### 2.3 Sample Size Calculation
```
Desired Sample Size = 300 firms

Power Analysis:
- Effect size (Cohen's f²): 0.15 (medium effect)
- Alpha level: 0.05
- Power (1-β): 0.80
- Number of predictors: 15
- Minimum required sample: 139 firms
- Target sample (accounting for 30% non-response): 300 firms
```

### 2.4 Sampling Strategy
**Stratified Random Sampling**
- **Stratum 1**: Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) - 200 firms (67%)
  - 10-249 employees
  - Assets < 1 billion Naira
- **Stratum 2**: Large Enterprises - 100 firms (33%)
  - 250+ employees  
  - Assets ≥ 1 billion Naira

**Subsector Representation**:
- Proportional allocation across 9 food processing subsectors
- Minimum 10 firms per subsector to enable subgroup analysis

## 3. PRIMARY DATA COLLECTION

### 3.1 Survey Instrument Development

#### 3.1.1 FDI Constructs (Adapted Scales)
**Knowledge Absorption Capacity** (Zahra & George, 2002)
- 4 items measuring acquisition, assimilation, transformation, and exploitation
- 5-point Likert scale (1=Strongly Disagree, 5=Strongly Agree)
- Cronbach's α target: > 0.70

**Task Performance** (Koopmans et al., 2013)  
- 4 items measuring work quality, efficiency, productivity, goal achievement
- 5-point Likert scale (1=Strongly Disagree, 5=Strongly Agree)
- Cronbach's α target: > 0.70

**Innovation Capability** (OECD Oslo Manual)
- 4 items covering product, process, marketing, and organizational innovation
- 5-point Likert scale (1=Very Low, 5=Very High)
- Cronbach's α target: > 0.70

#### 3.1.2 Firm Performance Measures
**Financial Performance**:
- Return on Investment (ROI) - self-reported percentage
- Return on Assets (ROA) - self-reported percentage
- Revenue growth rate - 3-year average

**Operational Performance**:
- Export intensity - percentage of revenue from exports
- Market share - estimated percentage in primary market
- Operational efficiency - 10-point self-assessment scale

**Verification Strategy**:
- Cross-check financial data with CBN/NBS databases where available
- Request supporting documents for large firms
- Use industry benchmarks for outlier detection

#### 3.1.3 Government Policy Perception
**Policy Effectiveness Dimensions** (7-point Likert scale):
- Tax incentives effectiveness (1=Very Ineffective, 7=Very Effective)
- Regulatory stability and predictability
- Infrastructure support quality
- Ease of doing business experience
- Corruption experience (reversed scale)
- Policy consistency over time
- Government support programs accessibility

#### 3.1.4 Firm Resources Assessment
**Human Capital**:
- Skilled labor ratio (percentage of workforce with tertiary education)
- Management experience (average years)
- Training investment (percentage of payroll)

**Technological Resources**:
- R&D expenditure (percentage of revenue)
- IoT/digital technology adoption (binary indicators)
- Technology partnerships (count and type)

**Financial Resources**:
- Liquidity ratio (current assets/current liabilities)
- Debt-to-equity ratio
- Access to credit (binary and ease rating)

### 3.2 Data Collection Process

#### 3.2.1 Survey Administration
**Mode**: Mixed-mode approach
- Primary: Computer-Assisted Personal Interview (CAPI)
- Secondary: Online survey (Qualtrics platform)
- Backup: Paper-based questionnaire

**Duration**: 45-60 minutes per interview

**Languages**: English (primary), Yoruba (translation available)

#### 3.2.2 Respondent Selection
**Target Respondents**:
- CEO/Managing Director (preferred)
- Operations Manager
- General Manager
- Finance Manager (for financial data verification)

**Respondent Criteria**:
- Minimum 2 years with the firm
- Decision-making authority or access to firm performance data
- Willing to provide informed consent

#### 3.2.3 Quality Control Measures
**Interviewer Training**:
- 2-day training program on survey administration
- Practice sessions with pilot firms
- Standardized protocols for data collection

**Data Quality Checks**:
- Real-time validation during data entry
- Consistency checks across related variables
- Follow-up calls for incomplete or inconsistent responses
- 10% random verification calls

## 5. DATA INTEGRATION METHODOLOGY

### 5.1 Matching Strategy
**Temporal Matching**:
- Match firm performance data to corresponding year's macro indicators
- Use lagged macro variables where theoretical justification exists
- Account for reporting delays in firm financial data

**Geographic Matching**:
- Lagos-specific indicators where available
- National indicators adjusted for regional variations
- Use Lagos State share of national totals for allocation

### 5.2 Variable Construction
**Composite Indices**:
- Knowledge Absorption Score: Average of 4 component items
- Policy Effectiveness Index: Weighted average of 7 policy dimensions
- Overall Performance Index: Normalized composite of 5 performance measures

**Interaction Terms**:
- FDI × Policy interactions for moderation analysis
- Firm characteristics × Macro environment interactions
- Time-varying interaction effects

### 5.3 Data Transformation
**Normalization**:
- Min-max scaling for composite indices (0-10 scale)
- Z-score standardization for regression analysis
- Log transformation for skewed financial variables

**Categorization**:
- Firm size categories based on Nigerian SMEDAN definitions
- FDI intensity quartiles for subgroup analysis
- Performance quintiles for comparative analysis

## 8. LIMITATIONS AND MITIGATION STRATEGIES

### 8.1 Methodological Limitations
**Cross-sectional Design**:
- **Limitation**: Cannot establish causality definitively
- **Mitigation**: Use lagged variables, instrumental variables, and theoretical justification

**Self-reported Performance Data**:
- **Limitation**: Potential bias in self-reported measures
- **Mitigation**: Triangulation with secondary data, objective measures where possible

**Sample Selection Bias**:
- **Limitation**: Participating firms may differ systematically from non-participants
- **Mitigation**: Compare sample characteristics with population, use propensity score methods

### 8.2 Data Quality Limitations
**Recall Bias**:
- **Limitation**: Historical performance data may be imprecise
- **Mitigation**: Focus on recent years, use multiple informants, cross-check with records

**Social Desirability Bias**:
- **Limitation**: Respondents may overstate positive outcomes
- **Mitigation**: Anonymous reporting, objective measures, validation questions

**Missing Data**:
- **Limitation**: Some firms may have incomplete financial records
- **Mitigation**: Multiple imputation techniques, sensitivity analysis

### 8.3 External Validity Limitations
**Geographic Scope**:
- **Limitation**: Results may not generalize beyond Lagos State
- **Mitigation**: Compare with national statistics, discuss generalizability explicitly

**Temporal Scope**:
- **Limitation**: Findings reflect specific time period and economic conditions
- **Mitigation**: Control for temporal factors, discuss contextual influences

**Sectoral Scope**:
- **Limitation**: Results specific to food processing sector
- **Mitigation**: Compare with other manufacturing sectors where data available
